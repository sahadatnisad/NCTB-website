# NCTB AI Learning Hub — Marketing Plan

> **Purpose.** How to get real students and teachers using the platform, cheaply, without funding — and how to build the *traction evidence* that later attracts company/investor money.
>
> **Honesty note:** I have not invented audience sizes, conversion rates, or costs. Where a number matters, I tell you to measure it. Bangladesh-specific figures (how many students search online, what parents pay for tuition, competitor pricing) must come from your own research and small tests, not from me.

---

## 1. Positioning — what you actually are

**One-line positioning:** *"A personal NCTB tutor in your pocket — learn the lesson, practise it, fix your mistakes, and prepare for the board exam, with an AI tutor when you're stuck."*

**Against the alternatives (verify each claim yourself before using in ads):**
- **vs. coaching centres / private tutors:** cheaper, available 24/7, patient, tracks your weak points. (Not a replacement for a great teacher — position as a *complement*, especially for students without tuition access.)
- **vs. free YouTube/PDF sites:** you don't just *show* content, you run a *learning loop* — practice, mistake tracking, spaced revision, mastery, and a tutor that answers *your* question.
- **vs. Shikkhok Batayon-style repositories:** those are content libraries; you are an adaptive learning *system* for students and an upskilling *system* for teachers. (Confirm their current features before comparing publicly.)

**Emotional core:** for the student who can't afford tuition, or studies alone at night — *you are on their side.* That's the brand feeling to protect in every message.

---

## 2. Audiences & who pays

Three distinct buyers, three messages:

1. **SSC/HSC students (older):** buy for themselves; motivated by board-exam fear and wanting to improve marks. Message: "Prepare smarter for your board exam." Channel: where teens are — Facebook, YouTube, TikTok, search.
2. **Parents (younger students, class 6–10):** *they* pay. Motivated by their child falling behind, tuition cost, wanting oversight. Message: "Give your child a tutor's attention for a fraction of tuition cost — and see their progress." Channel: Facebook groups, parent networks.
3. **Teachers:** buy for self-improvement / classroom help; also a *distribution channel* (a teacher who loves it brings students). Message: "Teach better, prepare faster, use AI in your classroom." Channel: teacher Facebook groups, school networks, word of mouth.

**Strategic note:** teachers are both a market *and* your cheapest acquisition channel for students. Winning teachers can be the flywheel.

---

## 3. The growth flywheel

```
   Free notes & board questions (SEO) ──▶ students arrive from search
                │                                   │
                ▼                                   ▼
        Genuinely useful free tier ───▶ some convert to paid (AI + full lessons)
                │                                   │
                ▼                                   ▼
     Teachers recommend to their classes ◀── teachers get free classroom value
                │
                ▼
     Word of mouth + testimonials ──▶ more free traffic ──▶ (loop)
```

The engine of the flywheel is **free value that ranks in search and gets shared**. Notes, board questions, and free videos are marketing *and* product at once.

---

## 4. Channels (ordered by cost-efficiency for an unfunded team)

### 4.1 SEO / organic search (highest ROI, slow build) — priority #1
- Every **note** and **board question** page targets a real student search query in Bangla/English.
- Publish consistently; internal-link notes → lessons → practice.
- Technical SEO: fast pages, sitemaps, schema.org educational markup, mobile-first (all in Build Phase 15).
- This compounds for free over years — perfect for a lifetime project. Start early; it takes months to rank.

### 4.2 YouTube (owned, compounding) — priority #2
- You're already producing video. A YouTube channel is *both* content hosting *and* a discovery engine.
- Free explainer videos → description links to the site → free notes → paid conversion.
- Consistency matters more than production polish. Solve real exam pain in each video.

### 4.3 Facebook groups & pages (where your audience already is) — priority #3
- Bangladeshi students, parents, and teachers are heavily on Facebook. Join/serve (don't spam) student and teacher groups; share genuinely useful free content.
- A Facebook page + occasional low-budget boosted posts to test messaging. **Measure cost-per-signup before scaling spend** — don't pour money in blind.

### 4.4 Teacher partnerships (your differentiator) — priority #4
- Offer teachers free classroom resources + free access in exchange for trying it with their class.
- A teacher recommending you to 40 students is the cheapest acquisition you'll ever get.
- Consider a simple referral: teachers (or students) who bring paying users get extended access.

### 4.5 School/community outreach — later
- Once proven, approach schools/NGOs, especially those serving students without tuition access. This is also a good *funding/impact* story.

### 4.6 Paid ads — only after the funnel converts organically
- Don't buy traffic until free traffic converts. Ads amplify a working funnel; they can't fix a broken one. When you test, start tiny and measure.

## 5. Launch sequence (matches Build Phases 14–20)

**Pre-launch (during Build Phase 14–15):**
- Build an email/Facebook waitlist with a landing page: "Free NCTB notes + AI tutor — join early."
- Publish 20–30 strong free notes + free board questions so there's real value on day one (and SEO starts indexing).
- Seed a YouTube channel with 5–10 videos.
- Recruit 5–10 friendly teachers and 20–50 students for a private beta.

**Soft launch (Build Phase 15):**
- Open to the waitlist + beta. Watch everything: where they drop off, what they'll pay for, what AI costs per user.
- Collect testimonials and real usage stories.

**Public launch (after the loop is proven):**
- Announce across channels. Lead with free value + a few authentic student results.
- Now begin small paid-ad tests if the funnel converts.

**Expansion (Build Phases 20+):**
- Each new subject = a content + marketing mini-launch (new notes ranking, new videos, targeted posts).

---

## 6. Content marketing = the product's free tier

You don't need a separate "content marketing team." Your **free notes, board questions, and free videos ARE the marketing.** Every free asset:
- ranks in search (acquisition),
- proves quality (trust),
- and leads to the paid loop (conversion).

So invest in making the free tier genuinely excellent — it's your cheapest, most durable growth engine.

---

## 7. Metrics that matter (measure from day one)

Don't vanity-track followers. Track the funnel:
- **Traffic → signup rate** (are visitors registering?)
- **Signup → activation** (do they complete a first lesson / find value?)
- **Activation → paid conversion** (what % pay, and for what?)
- **Retention** (do they come back in week 2, 4, 8? — *this is the single most important number for both product and funding*).
- **AI cost per active paid user** (protects your margins).
- **Channel cost-per-signup** (where to spend/not spend).
- **Teacher-driven signups** (is the flywheel real?).

Instrument these in Build Phase 15. Funders will ask for retention and unit economics above all.

---

## 8. Trust & brand safety (education is a trust business)
- Be honest in every claim (no fake results, no invented endorsements — also aligns with your own principles).
- Protect student privacy loudly (you have a zero-data-selling stance — market it).
- Label AI as assistive/formative, not a guarantee.
- Respond fast to support; a reputation for care spreads in tight student/teacher communities.

---

## 9. The funding narrative (build it into marketing from the start)

You want companies to eventually fund/pay. What they'll want to see (target, not promise):
- **Real, retained, paying users** across ≥2 subjects.
- **An active teacher base** (unique, defensible, hard to copy).
- **Healthy unit economics** (AI cost < revenue per user).
- **An impact story:** measurably helping students who lack access to tuition — strong for CSR-minded companies, EdTech investors, and development funders.
- **A content moat:** a large, high-quality, NCTB-aligned library that took years to build.

So: every month of good execution *is* fundraising. Keep clean metrics and a few powerful student stories; that deck writes itself once the numbers are real.

> **Honest caveat (again):** funding is never guaranteed and depends on factors outside your control (market conditions, investor appetite, execution). Treat traction as the goal in itself — a self-sustaining product is more valuable and more fundable than a big product waiting to be rescued.
